# Tips vs Triggers: The Conversion Copywriting Framework

Transform passive educational content into action-driving copy.

---

## Core Concept

**Tips** = educational content that sits passively. Feels helpful but doesn't create urgency. Gets bookmarked, saved for later, forgotten.

**Triggers** = psychological activators that expose problems, create discomfort, and compel immediate action. Makes the reader's current reality uncomfortable enough that staying put feels worse than taking action.

---

## The Psychology

### Loss Aversion
People are 2-3x more motivated to avoid losses than pursue gains. Trigger-based content focused on what readers are losing, missing, or doing wrong converts better than tips focused on what they could gain.

### Present Bias
People discount future benefits heavily compared to present problems. A problem that hurts NOW gets immediate action; a dream of future success gets postponed indefinitely.

### Gap Awareness
Most prospects aren't fully aware of the gap between where they are and where they need to be. Tips assume awareness. Triggers create awareness by illuminating the gap.

---

## The Fire Alarm Analogy

A helpful fire safety poster says: "In case of fire, stay calm and use the stairs."

A fire alarm says: "GET OUT NOW. THE BUILDING IS BURNING."

Which one makes you move?

Tips are the poster. Triggers are the alarm.

---

## Pattern Recognition

### Tips sound like:
- "How to..."
- "X ways to..."
- "The ultimate guide to..."
- "Steps to achieve..."
- "Best practices for..."

These create: "That's useful, I'll save this for later."

### Triggers sound like:
- "Why your [thing] is failing..."
- "The real reason [problem]..."
- "What you're losing every time..."
- "Stop [painful action]..."
- "The [mistake] that's costing you..."

These create: "Oh no, I need to fix this NOW."

---

## Transformation Examples

**Email Marketing:**
- TIP: "7 steps to grow your email list"
- TRIGGER: "Why your email list has flatlined (and why subscribers see your name and hit delete)"

**Course Creation:**
- TIP: "How to structure a profitable course"
- TRIGGER: "The course structure mistake bleeding your completion rates"

**Business Scaling:**
- TIP: "Build your dream business"
- TRIGGER: "Stop hemorrhaging money on ads that don't convert"

**Audience Building:**
- TIP: "Content strategies for attracting ideal clients"
- TRIGGER: "Your content is attracting broke browsers, not buyers. Here's why."

---

## The Bridge Framework (Sell the Crossing, Not the Bricks)

Don't list features. Sell the transformation.

**Formula:**
1. **WHO** is this for?
2. **WHERE** are they now? (current painful reality)
3. **WHERE** will they be after? (desired outcome)
4. **HOW** does this get them there? (mechanism, briefly)
5. **WHEN** can they start? (immediacy)

People don't buy courses, coaching, or templates. They buy the version of themselves on the other side.

---

## Pain Categories

When writing triggers, tap into one of these core pain types:

1. **Financial Pain**: Losing money, leaving money on the table, wasted ad spend
2. **Time Pain**: Hours wasted, inefficiency, doing things the hard way
3. **Status Pain**: Falling behind peers, looking amateur, missing out
4. **Uncertainty Pain**: Not knowing if it's working, guessing, lack of clarity

The more specific and quantifiable the pain, the stronger the trigger.

---

## Application by Format

### Headlines
Always use trigger framing. Tips get scrolled past.

### Bullets
Each bullet should trigger a specific pain or desire. Not: "Learn email marketing." Instead: "Stop sending emails that get ignored."

### Body Copy
Open with trigger (expose the problem), then transition to solution.

### CTAs
Frame around relief from pain, not just gaining benefit.
- Tip CTA: "Download the guide"
- Trigger CTA: "Stop losing leads today"

---

## The Golden Rule

Your content shouldn't make people think "that's useful."

It should make them think "I need to fix this NOW."

That's when they take action.

---

## Quick Audit

Before publishing any landing page copy, ask:

- [ ] Does this expose a problem or just explain a solution?
- [ ] Will this make someone uncomfortable about their current reality?
- [ ] Is the pain specific and quantifiable?
- [ ] Am I showing the crossing, not listing bricks?
- [ ] Does this create present urgency or future aspiration?
- [ ] Would someone save this for later (tip) or act on it now (trigger)?

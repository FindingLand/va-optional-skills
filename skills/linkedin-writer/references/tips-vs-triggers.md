# Tips vs Triggers: Conversion Copywriting Framework

## Core Concept: Why Tips Don't Convert

**Tips** are educational content that sits passively in the reader's mind. They feel helpful but don't create urgency. Tips get bookmarked, saved for later, and forgotten.

**Triggers** are psychological activators that expose problems, create discomfort, and compel immediate action. They make the reader's current reality uncomfortable enough that staying put feels worse than taking action.

## The Psychology

### Loss Aversion
Humans are 2-3x more motivated to avoid losses than pursue gains. Trigger-based content focused on what readers are *losing* converts better than tips focused on what they could *gain*.

### Present Bias
People discount future benefits heavily compared to present problems. A toothache at 3am gets immediate action; a dream of perfect teeth someday gets postponed. Make pain present and visceral, not future and abstract.

### The Fire Alarm Analogy
- **Tip (poster):** "In case of fire, stay calm and use the stairs."
- **Trigger (alarm):** "GET OUT NOW. THE BUILDING IS BURNING."

Which makes you move? In marketing, you need alarms.

## Pattern Recognition

### Tips sound like:
- "How to..."
- "X ways to..."
- "The ultimate guide to..."
- "Steps to achieve..."
- "Best practices for..."

Creates feeling: "That's useful, I'll save this for later."

### Triggers sound like:
- "Why your [thing] is failing..."
- "The real reason [problem]..."
- "What you're losing every time..."
- "Stop [painful action]..."
- "The [mistake] that's costing you..."

Creates feeling: "Oh fuck, I need to fix this NOW."

## Trigger Transformations

**Email Marketing:**
- TIP: "7 steps to grow your email list"
- TRIGGER: "Why your email list has flatlined (the real reason subscribers see your name and hit delete)"

**Course Creation:**
- TIP: "How to structure a profitable course"
- TRIGGER: "The course structure mistake that's bleeding your completion rates (and why students ask for refunds)"

**Audience Building:**
- TIP: "Content strategies for attracting your ideal client"
- TRIGGER: "Your content is attracting broke browsers, not buyers. Here's why."

## The Four Pain Categories

### 1. Financial Pain
Money being lost, wasted, or left on the table RIGHT NOW.
- "You're bleeding $X/month on ads that don't convert"
- "Every day without an email sequence is $XX in lost sales"

### 2. Time Pain
Hours wasted on inefficient processes or strategies that don't scale.
- "You're spending 40 hours/week on tasks that should take 10"
- "Every launch takes 6 months when it should take 6 weeks"

### 3. Status/Reputation Pain
Damage to credibility, respect, or professional standing.
- "Your students are complaining in Facebook groups"
- "Your audience sees you as just another generic coach"

### 4. Emotional Pain
Stress, overwhelm, impostor syndrome, fear of failure.
- "You're up at 3am worrying about next month's revenue"
- "You feel like a fraud every time you hit publish"

## Sell the Crossing, Not the Bricks

### The Problem with Feature-Based Selling

**SELLING BRICKS (Wrong):**
"Inside you'll get: 12 video modules, 47 templates, private community, weekly Q&A calls..."

Nobody wakes up thinking "I need 12 video modules." They wake up thinking "I need to make $10k this month."

**SELLING THE CROSSING (Right):**
"For beauty creators posting daily but making $0: Land your first $10k month using a simple 3-day email funnel. Works in 30 days even with a small audience."

### The Bridge Framework

1. **WHO**, Specific avatar with painful circumstances
2. **BEFORE**, Painful present (visceral and specific)
3. **AFTER**, Clear, measurable outcome
4. **HOW**, The method (simple, not comprehensive)
5. **WHEN**, Timeline that creates urgency

## The Specificity Principle

Vague triggers don't convert. Specific ones do.

- VAGUE: "Your business has problems"
- SPECIFIC: "Your 14% email open rate is costing you $3,200/month in lost sales"

- VAGUE: "You're not making enough money"
- SPECIFIC: "You're working 50-hour weeks to hit $5k months when you should be at $30k"

## The Nervousness Test

Good trigger content should make you slightly uncomfortable before publishing. If it feels too safe, too helpful, too nice, it's not a trigger.

You should feel a small pit in your stomach thinking "is this too direct?" That's the sweet spot.

## Quick Reference by Format

### Social Media Posts

**TIPS (Scrolled Past):**
- "5 ways to increase engagement"
- "Content ideas for course creators"

**TRIGGERS (Stop the Scroll):**
- "Your engagement tanked. Here's why."
- "Why your audience growth stalled at 2k"

### Email Subject Lines

**TIPS (Low Opens):**
- "7 email tips for better engagement"
- "Best practices for email marketing"

**TRIGGERS (High Opens):**
- "Why 87% of your list ignores you"
- "The subject line mistake costing you $X/month"

## Conversion Checklist

Before publishing, ask:

- [ ] Does this expose a problem or just explain a solution?
- [ ] Will this make someone uncomfortable about their current reality?
- [ ] Is the pain specific and quantifiable?
- [ ] Am I showing the crossing or listing the bricks?
- [ ] Does this create present urgency or future aspiration?
- [ ] Would someone save this for later (tip) or act now (trigger)?
- [ ] Am I slightly nervous to publish this?

## The Golden Rule

Your content shouldn't make people think "that's useful."

It should make them think "oh fuck, I need to fix this NOW."

That's when they buy.

# Copywriting Best Practices

Persuasion techniques and frameworks for high-converting copy.

---

## Persuasion Techniques

### 1. Specificity
Vague claims are forgettable. Specific details build credibility.
- Weak: "Grow your business"
- Strong: "Add $2,847/month in recurring revenue"

### 2. "You" Language
Make it about the reader, not about you.
- Weak: "I created this course to help entrepreneurs"
- Strong: "You'll finally stop guessing and start scaling"

### 3. Numbers
Specific numbers catch attention and create tangible value.
- Odd numbers (3, 5, 7) often stand out more
- "7 proven templates" beats "several templates"

### 4. "Because"
Adding a reason makes requests more persuasive.
- "Download now because this offer expires Friday"
- "Sign up today because spots are limited"

### 5. Social Proof
Showing others benefit persuades new prospects to follow suit.
- "Trusted by over 10,000 entrepreneurs"
- "Join 500+ course creators who've already transformed their launches"
- Testimonials, case studies, user counts

### 6. Curiosity Gap
Leave out key details to pique interest and drive action.
- "The one mistake killing your conversions (hint: it's not your offer)"
- "What 8-figure creators know that you don't"

### 7. Loss Framing
People are 2-3x more motivated to avoid losses than pursue gains.
- "Stop losing $500/month to broken funnels"
- Frame what they'll lose by NOT acting

### 8. Scarcity & Urgency
Limited availability prompts quicker decisions.
- "Only 20 spots available"
- "$197 today, $497 tomorrow"
- Only use when TRUE — fake scarcity destroys trust

### 9. Fresh Starts
Associating with new beginnings is motivating.
- "Make this the quarter you finally nail your launches"
- "Start your transformation today"

### 10. Aspirational Identity
Help audience identify with who they want to become.
- "Join the ranks of profitable course creators"
- "Become the go-to expert in your niche"

### 11. Effort Highlighting
Showing effort increases perceived value.
- "Based on 500+ hours of testing across 12 niches"
- "We spent 3 years perfecting this system"

### 12. Questions
Questions provoke curiosity and encourage self-reflection.
- "What would you do with an extra 10 hours per week?"
- "Ready to stop fighting with your tech stack?"

## Frameworks

### PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution)

1. **Problem:** Pinpoint a core pain point
2. **Agitate:** Describe the pain/consequences emotionally
3. **Solution:** Present your offer as the relief

Example:
> Launching a course only to hear crickets on cart open — it's gut-wrenching. You pour weeks into creation, write the sales page, send the emails... and then nothing. I know that feeling. But what if your next launch had a waitlist before you even finished recording?

### AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action)

1. **Attention:** Hook/headline that grabs
2. **Interest:** Interesting facts or story that pulls them in
3. **Desire:** Benefits, testimonials, vision of outcome
4. **Action:** Clear CTA

### The Bridge Framework

Connect current state to desired state:
1. **WHO** this is for
2. **WHERE** they are now (pain)
3. **WHERE** they'll be after (transformation)
4. **HOW** this gets them there (mechanism)
5. **WHEN** they can start (immediacy)

## Voice of Customer (VOC)

Use the exact words your audience uses to describe their problems.

If they say "I'm drowning in tech" — use that phrase, not "I lack an automation solution."

**How to gather VOC:**
- Survey your audience
- Read their comments and forum posts
- Get on calls and listen
- Note exact phrases and mirror them back

---

## Objection Handling

Anticipate hesitations and address them proactively.

**Common objections:**
- "I can't afford it"
- "Will this work for my situation?"
- "I don't have time"
- "I've tried things like this before"

**How to address:**
- Empathize first: "I know investing in another program feels risky..."
- Counter with story/proof: "Let me share how Sarah recouped her investment in 2 weeks..."
- Reframe: "Think you don't have time? Most of my busiest clients felt the same — until they realized this actually saves time..."

---

## CTA Best Practices

- One primary CTA per section
- Make it visually obvious
- Action-oriented: "Download the guide," "Reserve my seat," "Yes, I want in!"
- Repeat at key decision points
- Don't muddy with multiple competing asks

## Structural Techniques

### Visual Rhythm
- Vary line length
- Short paragraphs (1-3 sentences)
- Strategic white space
- Bold sparingly for emphasis

### The P.S.
Many readers scroll to P.S. first. Use it to:
- Restate the main CTA
- Add urgency
- Include a bonus thought

### One Reader
Write to one specific person, not a crowd. Use "you" not "people" or "entrepreneurs."
